The ten requirements

Discovery
R1 · Can a program enumerate the definitions?
R2 · Are aggregation, grain, slicing dimensions, and time behaviour declared?
Trust
R3 · Are provenance and lineage exposed?
R4 · Does freshness travel where the consumer reads it?
R6 · Is certification machine-visible?
Enforcement and exchange
R5 · Is access enforced at query time for the caller’s own identity?
R7 · Does the result carry units, grain, and caveats?
R8 · Are invalid requests rejected in structured form?
Identity and lifecycle
R9 · Are versioning and deprecation signalled in pollable form?
R10 · Does metric identity survive a rename?

On the PromptQL sample the coverage statement sub-check inside R7 reads declared, from its reviewed context, even though the full result contract is not documented. R8 is not assessable on both, by design: structured rejection is runtime behaviour that project files cannot evidence.

Why PromptQL fits the framework

PromptQL’s shared context is reviewed business definitions and assumptions saved for agents to apply. In contract terms, that is declared semantics with a review trail: R2 evidence, R3 provenance, and the closest thing in the field to R6 certification. Its metadata permissions speak to R5. And the coverage question that opened the conversation, whether an answer states the population it represents, is carried in the checker as a named sub-check of R7, the coverage statement.
